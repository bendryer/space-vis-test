Explore the OUniverse

Since Colin Pillinger’s appointment in 1983, the Open University’s School of Physical Sciences has built a heritage in space instrumentation and science.

From rovers and landers on other worlds to telescopes imaging distant planets, stars and galaxies, and satellites monitoring our Earth, the OU has played a part in a wide range of spacecraft. Explore of them in the OUniverse.

It takes a planet to explore the Solar System. Each mission is the shared work of thousands of brilliant people at space agencies, universities and companies around the world, and we’re proud and grateful to explore alongside every one of them.

The research you’ve been exploring is taught by the people who do it, and you can study it with us from wherever you are.

Study with us

Our BSc (Honours) Natural Sciences (Astronomy and Planetary Science) (opens in new tab) is built on this research, with modules including Astronomy (S284) (opens in new tab) and Planetary science and the search for life (S283) (opens in new tab), which becomes Planetary science (S287) from October 2027. At postgraduate level, Space science (S818) (opens in new tab) puts real data from missions such as Apollo, Rosetta, Curiosity and Gaia in your hands.

Learning by doing, from home

You study part-time, around work and life, and most of our undergraduate study needs no previous qualifications. On Remote experiments in physics and space (SXPS288) (opens in new tab) you control real equipment over the internet, from our PIRATE telescope in Tenerife to spectrometers in Milton Keynes, using technology OU researchers flew on ESA’s Rosetta mission. Students on our MPhys Astrophysics with Space Science pathway (opens in new tab) and MSc Space Science and Technology (opens in new tab) go one step further, driving a robotic rover across our Mars Yard from their own homes.

Space for everyone

Space doesn’t belong to science alone. It has inspired art, music, stories and ideas for as long as people have looked up, and that curiosity runs right across the OU, from the arts and humanities to technology, business and the social sciences. Whatever you study with us, there’s room to reach for the stars.
