### BepiColombo [D 607]
BepiColombo is a joint mission between ESA and JAXA that is due to enter orbit around Mercury on 21 November 2026 and to be fully commissioned by April 2027. The mission uses two separate orbiters to perform the most comprehensive study of the planet's surface, composition, and magnetic field to date. The model shows the moment JAXA's Mio orbiter is released; after that, the MOSIF sunshield separates and ESA's Mercury Planetary Orbiter (MPO) flies free. By mapping the planet at various wavelengths, scientists aim to solve the mystery of Mercury's oversized core and its richness in volatile materials.
### LUPEX [D 530]
The Lunar Polar Exploration (LUPEX) mission is a joint JAXA-ISRO mission to investigate water and other resources in the Moon's south polar region. Currently targeted for launch no earlier than 2028, it will use a mobile rover to explore areas with differing illumination, temperature and geology, investigating the distribution, abundance and physical state of lunar water at several sites and at depths of up to about 1.5 metres. The rover is planned to operate for about 3.5 months after landing, and possibly for up to a year.
### Shackleton Rover [D 506]
Taking inspiration from Sir Ernest Shackleton's 1914 expedition, and in coordination with his family, Shackleton Rover is a proposed UK-led mission to the lunar south pole. It would showcase and develop the UK's technological, scientific and industrial capabilities in space exploration while inspiring the next generation of space scientists and engineers. By establishing a long-lived experiment on the Moon, it would also give the UK a voice in the responsible exploration of the Moon and its resources.
### Rosetta [D 493]
The Rosetta mission was a ground-breaking ESA project that performed the first successful long-term study of a comet's nucleus. It orbited Comet 67P for over two years, observing its transformation as it travelled through the inner Solar System. It also deployed a lander, Philae, to study the surface in situ, providing ground truth for the orbiter's measurements. The mission provided unprecedented data on the physical and chemical properties of comets and their role in the origin of life.
### Didymos [D 491]
Didymos is a near-Earth asteroid about 800 metres across, shaped like a spinning top, that turns once every 2.26 hours. It has a small moon, Dimorphos. In September 2022 NASA's DART spacecraft deliberately crashed into Dimorphos and shortened its orbit around Didymos by about 33 minutes, the first test of moving an asteroid. ESA's Hera mission arrives in late 2026 to measure exactly what the impact did. The shapes shown here are the models DART's team built from the spacecraft's images.
### Dimorphos [D 497]
Dimorphos is Didymos's small moon, about 177 metres across, circling it about 1.1 kilometres away every 11 hours 22 minutes. On 26 September 2022 NASA's DART spacecraft hit it at about 6 kilometres per second, shortening its orbit by about 33 minutes and throwing out a long trail of debris. Before the impact it kept the same face towards Didymos; Hera will find out whether the impact has left it wobbling. The shape shown is DART's model from the images taken in the spacecraft's final minutes.
